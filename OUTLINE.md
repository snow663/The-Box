# The Box — Working Outline

## Opening — You Are Already Inside
The reader makes an apparently obvious judgment before discovering that the judgment depends on relationships among things already accepted as true.

## Part I — Building the Box
1. **The First Wall — Knowing Creates Boundaries** — Knowledge eliminates possibilities and enables prediction.
2. **Everything Is Relative to What's in the Box** — Facts acquire meaning through relationships.
3. **The Invisible Wall** — A model of reality quietly becomes mistaken for reality.
4. **Why the Box Works** — The Box is a necessary feature of intelligence, not a defect.

## Part II — The Moving Contents
5. **Nothing Enters Alone** — New facts alter existing relationships.
6. **Reinterpretation** — New information can change what old observations mean.
7. **Error Propagation** — A mistaken inference can recursively reorganize later knowledge.
8. **The Coherent Lie** — Internal coherence is not identical to external truth.
9. **Intellectual Version Control** — Preserve the distinction between observation, inference, and dependent conclusions.

## Part III — When Something Lands Outside
10. **The Anomaly** — Most anomalies fail. The important case is the one that survives.
11. **Impossible Relative to What?** — Incompatibility with a model is not necessarily incompatibility with reality.
12. **Defending the Walls** — Interconnected knowledge naturally resists disruptive evidence.
13. **Rearranging the Box** — Paradigm shifts reorganize old facts rather than merely adding new ones.

## Part IV — The Unthinkable Becomes True
14. **How Did They Know?** — Ancient accounts as an extreme stress test of modern plausibility.
15. **The Bargain of Skepticism** — Extraordinary evidence must be allowed to support extraordinary conclusions.
16. **The Humiliation of Certainty** — The shock of discovering that a boundary belonged to us, not reality.

## Part V — Boxes Within Boxes
17. **The Intelligent Prison** — Individuals, disciplines, cultures, and civilizations build nested models.
18. **The Universe Problem** — We cannot completely step outside the system used to understand the system.
19. **The Horizon Is Not the Edge** — A limit of observation is not necessarily a limit of existence.
20. **The Box Containing the Box** — This philosophy must remain subject to its own criticism.

## Ending — Leave the Walls Visible
The goal is not to think without a Box. It is to know that one exists, preserve the difference between observation and inference, and remain capable of rearranging the model when reality demands it.

> Do not immediately believe the impossible thing. Do not immediately reject it. Measure it.
