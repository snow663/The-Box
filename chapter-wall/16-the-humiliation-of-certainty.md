# Chapter 16 — The Humiliation of Certainty

## Core claim
The deepest intellectual shock is not discovering a surprising fact; it is discovering that a boundary we attributed to reality belonged to our model.

## Material on the wall
- “We mistook the boundary of our knowledge for the boundary of reality.”
- Worse: “We didn’t know we’d done it.”
- Humiliation can be productive: it restores distinction between confidence and certainty.
- Avoid anti-science framing; self-correction is one of science’s strengths.

## Questions
- Find cases where correction increased rather than diminished confidence in the method.
