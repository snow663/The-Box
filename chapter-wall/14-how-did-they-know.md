# Chapter 14 — How Did They Know?

## Core claim
Use an ancient account as an extreme stress test: what happens epistemically if something modern people dismissed as impossible acquires rigorous physical support?

## Material on the wall
- Noah/flood account can be the primary thought experiment, but this chapter is not religious apologetics.
- Ancient people were cognitively modern: capable of history, fiction, propaganda, allegory, error, and observation.
- “Ancient” does not imply literal truth; neither does it imply fabrication.
- Hypothetical evidence can include archaeology, geology, chemistry, dating, genetics, independent sites/methods.
- If the physical event becomes difficult to deny, theological interpretation remains a separate question.
- The destabilizing question becomes: **How did they know?**
- **We may have mistaken our inability to explain an ancient claim for evidence that the people making it couldn’t possibly have been telling us something they believed was real.**

## Guardrails
- Clearly distinguish actual evidence from hypothetical stress test.
- Research all real-world claims before publication.
