# Chapter 9 — Intellectual Version Control

## Core claim
We should preserve provenance: what was observed, what was inferred, what assumptions were used, and which later conclusions depend on them.

## Material on the wall
- Intellectual equivalent of source control.
- Mark claims as observation / interpretation / assumption / dependent conclusion.
- When a premise changes, we should be able to identify what needs reevaluation.
- Git repository for *The Box* is itself an experiment in this principle.
- **DO NOT manufacture the example.** Let manuscript development produce a real case.
- Later insert a genuine excerpt from this repository’s commit history showing an idea being introduced, changed, moved, rejected, or resurrected.
- Do not sanitize embarrassing mistakes merely to make development look prescient.
- Possible joke:
  > We believe intellectual honesty requires preserving what we knew, inferred, and later discovered was wrong. Accordingly, we deleted all evidence that we were ever wrong. Please enjoy Chapter 9.
- A philosophy about uncertainty ought to have scars.

## Evidence to collect later
- Commit(s) where an assumption caused downstream changes.
- Original text, revision, reason, and resulting reorganization.
