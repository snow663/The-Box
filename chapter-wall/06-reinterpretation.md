# Chapter 6 — Reinterpretation

## Core claim
New knowledge can leave an old observation untouched while radically changing what we think that observation means.

## Material on the wall
- Observation and interpretation must be separated.
- Scientific revolutions often do not prove earlier measurements false; they alter the framework connecting them.
- Old evidence can become new evidence without changing physically.
- A correct anomaly may initially look wrong because surrounding facts were interpreted under an older assumption.

## Questions
- Candidate historical examples: continental drift/plate tectonics; ulcers and H. pylori; meteorites; deep time.
- Research examples rigorously before committing them to manuscript.
