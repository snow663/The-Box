# Chapter 1 — The First Wall: Knowing Creates Boundaries

## Core claim
Knowledge does two things simultaneously: it expands what we can understand and narrows what we consider possible.

## Material on the wall
- Ignorance leaves possibility open; knowledge gives possibility shape.
- Every successful prediction strengthens an invisible boundary between *can happen* and *cannot happen*.
- The transition to watch:
  - “We have never observed this.”
  - “This does not happen.”
- Knowledge is not the villain. A useful model *must* exclude possibilities or it predicts nothing.
- Possible line: **The greatest limitation of human knowledge may not be ignorance. It may be knowledge itself.**
- Possible line: **Ignorance leaves the world open. Knowledge gives it shape.**
- Possible line: **What began as a description of everything we know quietly becomes a boundary around everything we believe can exist.**

## Questions
- What historical/scientific example best shows a previously sensible boundary later failing?
- How early should we distinguish uncertainty from “anything goes”?
