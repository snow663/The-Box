# Loose Piece — Thinking Outside the Box

> **Likely homes:** Chapter 18, Chapter 20, and/or the ending. Preserve as modular material until manuscript assembly.

“Think outside the box” is one of those phrases that sounds profound until you actually think outside the box about thinking outside the box.

Suppose you manage it.

Congratulations.

You're outside the Box.

Now what?

You haven't escaped having a model of reality. You've constructed a larger one.

So there you stand in your exciting new intellectual space, feeling liberated, while behind you sits the old Box containing everything you previously knew.

And now you have a logistical problem.

Do you unpack it?

Some of that stuff is useful.

Gravity probably still works. Might want to keep that.

Arithmetic has been pretty dependable.

Fire remains hot enough that throwing out the entire concept because we're feeling intellectually adventurous seems unnecessarily ambitious.

So you start unpacking.

Gravity goes over here. Cause and effect can stay. Mathematics against that wall. A few assumptions get thrown away. Some old certainties go into a pile marked **PROBABLY SHOULD HAVE QUESTIONED THIS EARLIER.**

Eventually the new room starts looking comfortable.

You organize things.

You establish relationships.

You figure out where everything belongs.

You develop increasingly reliable expectations about how things behave in this larger space.

And then, inevitably, somebody points at a door.

Shit.

There's another room.

This is the problem with “thinking outside the box.”

You never really get outside.

You move into a bigger Box.

Worse, you brought most of your shit with you.

And this creates an interesting choice every time our understanding expands.

Do we unpack the old Box immediately and arrange everything exactly as it was?

Do we shove the entire thing into a corner labeled **PREVIOUSLY ESTABLISHED KNOWLEDGE** and start filling the new room from scratch?

Neither works particularly well.

Unpack too quickly and the old arrangement quietly dictates the new one. The larger room becomes nothing more than the old room with additional floor space.

Refuse to unpack at all and you discard enormous amounts of knowledge simply because they were discovered under an incomplete model.

The sensible approach is considerably more annoying.

You open the old Box one piece at a time.

You ask what each thing actually is.

Was this observed?

Was it inferred?

Was it assumed?

Did we measure it, or did we conclude it because everything else in the old room seemed to require it?

Does it still belong where we put it?

Most of the contents survive.

Some move.

A few turn out to be things we've been hauling around for generations because nobody remembered who packed them.

And occasionally, buried underneath everything else, we find something particularly embarrassing:

an assumption labeled as a fact.

That may be the real meaning of intellectual progress.

Not escaping the Box.

Moving into a larger one without automatically arranging the furniture the same way.

Because every attempt to escape the Box constructs another Box.

The trick isn't getting out.

It's remembering, every time you move, that you still have walls.
